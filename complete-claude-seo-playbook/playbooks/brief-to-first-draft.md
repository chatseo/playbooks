# Turn an approved brief into a first draft

**Area:** Content strategy and briefs · **Time:** 1 h · **Skill:** `first-draft`

**Goal.** Produce a complete first draft that follows the brief section by section and reads like the site, not like a model.

**When.** A brief is approved and you would rather edit a draft than write it from a blank page.

## You need

- the approved brief with sections, word targets and required specifics
- two or three published pages from the site that sound the way you want, pasted in full
- the list of facts and numbers the draft is allowed to use

## Steps

1. Paste the sample pages into Claude first and ask it to describe the voice in five rules, such as sentence length, use of we, and how claims are backed.
2. Paste the brief and the allowed facts and ask Claude to write the draft one section at a time, in that voice, using only the facts provided.
3. Tell Claude that where the brief demands a number or an example you did not supply, it writes the placeholder NEEDS INPUT instead of inventing one.
4. Claude returns the draft with every section at its word target and every NEEDS INPUT placeholder listed at the top for you to fill.
5. Fill each placeholder from your own data, cut any sentence that could sit on a competitor's page unchanged, and pass the draft to editing.

**Done when the draft has zero NEEDS INPUT placeholders left and every section matches the brief's word target within 20 percent.**

## Rules

- Claude never states a number it was not given. It writes "cannot compute from this data" instead of estimating.
- Every step ends on one action, not a list of options.
