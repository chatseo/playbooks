# Add real experience to a draft

**Area:** Content strategy and briefs · **Time:** 45 min · **Skill:** `eeat-injector`

**Goal.** Rework a draft so it shows first-hand experience and expertise a reader and a rater can see, not just claim.

**When.** A draft is technically correct but could have been written by anyone who never did the thing.

## You need

- the draft, pasted in full
- raw material from someone who did the work: call notes, screenshots described in words, numbers from a real project, or a short interview transcript
- the author's name and the one credential that matters for this topic

## Steps

1. Interview the person who did the work for 15 minutes and write down every specific, a date, a number, a mistake, a tool, a result, into one notes file.
2. Paste the draft and the notes into Claude and ask it to mark every paragraph that makes a claim without a first-hand detail behind it.
3. Ask Claude to rewrite only the marked paragraphs, weaving in a detail from the notes, and to leave paragraphs alone where the notes have nothing relevant.
4. Claude returns the revised draft with each change listed, and a short list of paragraphs it could not fix because the notes had nothing, for you to cut or source.
5. Add an author line with the credential and cut the paragraphs Claude could not fix rather than leaving unsupported claims in.

**Done when every claim in the draft is backed by a first-hand detail or has been cut, and the author line is in place.**

## Rules

- Claude never states a number it was not given. It writes "cannot compute from this data" instead of estimating.
- Every step ends on one action, not a list of options.
