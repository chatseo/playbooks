For the sake of this masterclass, let's take a dentist: a practice in Ballwin, Missouri, 700 Google reviews, #20 in its own city's map pack. Here's why, and how to fix it.

Step 1Benchmark four search surfaces before you touch anything

The instinct when you open a new site is to start fixing. Don't. Not even the obvious mistake you just spotted.

Take a snapshot first. If you fix things before you measure, you can never prove what worked. That matters for your own learning, and ten times more if a client is paying you a retainer.

And the snapshot can't be "where do we rank on Google". For local, you track four surfaces: the map pack (with a geo-grid across the city), organic rankings, whether the business is named in AI answers, and which third-party sites those AI answers cite. That last list becomes your to-do list in step 3. Keep it.

Then install the three basics: Google Search Console, Google Analytics 4, and call tracking. For a local business, a call is the conversion. If you don't track calls, you're flying blind on the one metric the owner cares about.

Step 2Fix the Google Business Profile first, because it's the fastest lever you have

The practice is located in Ballwin. It serves Ballwin. But the profile was optimized for Chesterfield, the neighboring city.

So Google had a practice in Ballwin telling it, in effect, "I'm about Chesterfield". Result: #20 at home. The fix takes seconds. Point the profile at the city where you actually are.

Nobody should promise results in SEO. But this pattern shows up again and again, and fixing it alone often moves a profile 5 to 10 spots in the map pack. Same for the primary category: the wrong category is the other one-second fix that moves rankings. Here the category was already right, so location was the whole problem.

Why it's so powerful: Google's local ranking comes down to relevance, distance and prominence. This practice already had prominence (700 reviews). It was losing on relevance to its own city. You don't need more reviews when the signal pointing Google at the right city is broken.

After the fix, the routine is simple: keep driving Google reviews consistently, not in bursts. Add photos of the office, the team, the equipment. Recheck location and category every time someone touches the profile. Most people overcomplicate the GBP. Get the location and category right, then feed it reviews.

Step 3Find the 4 to 7 directories that AI engines actually cite

This is where the playbook breaks from how local SEO worked for a decade.

The old way: build citations in bulk. Submit to 100+ directories, tick the box, move on. Honestly, it barely mattered. Citations matter again. But not as a volume game.

AI engines answer "best dentist in Ballwin" by retrieving pages and citing them. Go back to the AI citations from step 1 and look at which third-party sites appear. For this dentist, four came up: HealthGrades, Delta Dental (the insurer's dentist directory), Yelp, and ADA.org. That's the list. Not 150 directories. Four.

Across most local industries, 4 to 7 citation sources actually show up. The rest are probably not worth your time. And the list is different for every niche: a plumber's won't include HealthGrades, a lawyer's will include sites a dentist never needs. You don't guess it. You read it off the AI citations.

Step 4Do SEO on those directories, not just on your website

Being listed on HealthGrades isn't enough. You need to rank inside HealthGrades.

Search the directory for dentists in the area and the practice is buried far down the list. So when an AI engine retrieves that page, it sees other brands first. Three moves to fix it.

1. Consider the sponsored slots. The HealthGrades results page had 3 sponsored listings at the top. AI engines don't care whether a listing is paid. They see the brands at the top of the page. If the directory is a top citation in your niche, a sponsored slot might be one of the cheapest AI visibility plays available.

2. Split your review effort 90/10. 90% of your review requests go to Google. The other 10% go to these secondary review surfaces. That's how you climb inside the directory and give AI engines fresh signals from a source they already trust.

3. Rewrite the profile like a web page. The practice's HealthGrades profile was thin: strip the boilerplate and one sentence was left. Treat it exactly like a page on your site. Get the topics and entities from the pages already ranking for "cosmetic dentist Ballwin", draft around 500 words, compress to 300 or 150 if the platform has a limit, and make sure each service (veneers, implants, Invisalign) is covered naturally.

Step 5Audit your brand's footprint for inconsistencies

AI engines build their picture of your business from everything written about it online. If that picture is contradictory, you lose trust and visibility.

For this practice, the audit found something no rank tracker would: the practice had changed hands. The current dentist took it over, and the web was still full of the previous dentist's info. Old names, old details, scattered across directories and profiles.

That's a massive to-do list from one prompt. Clean it up source by source, starting with the sites that appear in AI answers or on page 1.

Step 5 · Paste this into ChatSEO

List every inconsistency you find in business name, address, phone, hours, practitioner or owner name, services and website URL, across directories, review sites, social profiles, maps and news. For each one: the source URL, what it says, the correct value, and a priority (high if the source appears in AI answers or on Google page 1). Flag anything linked to a previous owner, old address or old phone number.

Step 6Clean up the technical and on-page basics

This site wasn't in bad shape. Two issues stood out, and they're the same two you'll find on most local sites.

Over-optimized title tags. "Dental Bonding in Ballwin, Missouri" is fine. Then came variations stacking the city, the state abbreviation and the ZIP code. The rule: one clean version. "Dental Bonding in Ballwin, MO | Practice Name". LLMs and Google both understand that "Ballwin, Missouri", "Ballwin MO" and the ZIP refer to the same place. Stuffing all three adds nothing, and it probably hurts.

Boilerplate meta descriptions. Many pages shared the same generic one. In 2026 there's no excuse: every page can have a unique description in minutes.

One rule most people miss. Don't put your phone number in the title or meta description. If the searcher can grab your number from the snippet, they don't click, and organic CTR feeds rankings. This only applies to organic results: on your Google Business Profile, the phone number obviously stays.

Step 7Build one page per service + city, and optimize it properly

The practice already had the right structure: "Invisalign in Ballwin, Missouri". Service plus core location. Keep that structure.

The problem was the optimization. The homepage had too many words and was aggressive without being well optimized. A good local page has a word count within the median range of the competitors ranking for that keyword (not double, not half), covers all the critical topics and entities for that service in that location, and is optimized for one primary topic and one location.

The workflow: look at what ranks for "cosmetic dentist Ballwin Missouri", list what those pages cover and how long they are, then write a page that matches the range and fills the gaps. Step 8Write local content, not national content

This is the mistake almost every local blog makes.

The practice's blog had articles like "How long do dental implants last?". That's a national question. Thousands of sites answer it, AI answers it directly, and it does nothing for a practice that only serves Ballwin.

The rule: every piece of content should build relevance for your location and your expertise. What that looks like in practice: statistics-driven, local linkbait. Content built on Ballwin-specific data that local news, bloggers and organizations would want to cite. Some ideas will be good, some won't. But ideas are no longer the bottleneck. Execution is.

Local links build local topical authority, the signal that says "this business belongs to Ballwin".

Then you do the part AI can't: reach out, sponsor, show up.

Why the order matters

  1. Benchmark four surfaces (so you can prove every gain)
  2. Fix GBP location and category (fastest lever, biggest jump)
  3. Find the 4 to 7 directories AI cites (from data, not a template)
  4. Rank inside those directories (sponsored slots, 90/10 reviews, rewritten profiles)
  5. Clean up brand inconsistencies (one coherent story for AI engines)
  6. Fix titles and metas (no ZIP stuffing, no phone numbers)
  7. Build and optimize service + city pages (before any blog post)
  8. Write local, data-driven content (never national topics)
  9. Build local links (topical authority tied to your city)

It runs from cheapest and fastest to slowest and most compounding. Steps 1 to 4 can move rankings within weeks. Steps 7 to 9 are what keep you there when competitors wake up. And steps 3 and 4 only exist because you tracked AI citations in step 1.

The honest caveats

  • This is one site. A live teardown, not a case study with results. The 5 to 10 spot lift is a pattern seen across many campaigns, not a measured outcome on this practice.
  • CTR as a ranking factor. Google denied it for years; its 2023 antitrust testimony described NavBoost, which uses click data. Directionally it holds, but don't treat it as settled.
  • "AI engines don't care if a listing is sponsored." Plausible, no hard data yet. Test one directory before you spend on all of them.
  • The directory list is niche-specific. HealthGrades and Delta Dental are dentist answers. Pull your own list.
