The 4 steps

You can start the first one this afternoon.

1

List 20 past clients whose site is still live

Straight from your invoices. Put anyone who ever asked "do you also do SEO?" at the top — they're pre-sold. Don't over-qualify the rest; half the yeses come from names you'd have bet against.

2

Run this on their site

Paste it into ChatSEO with their URL. You get three findings and one headline sentence — that's the whole email.

The reopening brief 
I built this website for a client and I want to reopen the
relationship with an SEO offer. Site: [URL]

Read the live site. If you can't load it, say so and stop.

Give me exactly THREE findings, chosen because a non-technical
business owner would immediately understand why they cost money.
Prefer: a service page that targets no searched term, a page
that should exist and doesn't, a title losing clicks it already
earns.

For each: what it is in one plain sentence with no SEO jargon |
which page | what customers are typing instead | what I'd change.

Never invent a search volume, ranking or traffic figure. If you
don't have the number, write "I'd need Search Console access to
size this" — do not estimate it.

Then write ONE headline sentence for the top of an email: the
single most valuable thing this site is missing, phrased as an
opportunity, not a criticism. It must not imply the site was
built badly.

Do not write the email. Do not list alternatives.
3

Send this email

No deck, no attachment, no calendar link. Five a day is thirty minutes — two weeks is fifty clients.

The email 
Subject: something I noticed on [site]

Hi [name],

I was looking at [site] the other day — still looks great.

One thing stood out: [headline sentence from the brief].

Here are the three things I'd fix first:

1. [finding one]
2. [finding two]
3. [finding three]

I've started offering SEO alongside the builds, so I can take
care of this properly if you want. Either way the list above
is yours.

Want me to walk you through it on a quick call?

[you]
4

Quote a monthly price on the reply

Not a project fee. One page, three tiers, prices below — most people take the middle one, which is why there are three.

The one line not to cut: "either way the list above is yours." Giving the findings away unconditionally is what makes the email a favour instead of a pitch. And keep the offer to one sentence in the middle — never the subject, never a P.S. with a price in it.

What you deliver every month

Roughly 4–5 hours per client, once you've done it twice.

Monthly reportWhat you did, what moved, what's next15 min

The report is the one that matters. Every client who's been burned by an SEO agency was burned the same way — they paid for months and could never tell whether anything happened. Send it even in a flat month: say it was flat, say what you did, say what's next. Clients don't churn on bad numbers, they churn on silence.

  • Never price by the hour. That's freelancing with extra admin, and every efficiency you gain becomes a pay cut.

  • Never promise a position or a date. Promise the work, the reporting, and an honest read of what moved.
