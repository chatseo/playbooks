The 4 steps

You can start the first one this afternoon.

1

List 20 past clients whose site is still live

Straight from your invoices. Put anyone who ever asked "do you also do SEO?" at the top — they're pre-sold. Don't over-qualify the rest; half the yeses come from names you'd have bet against.

2

Run this on their site

One line, their domain. This is SEO research mode — you don't need to connect anything, so it works on any site, including ones you haven't touched in two years.

The prompt 
I need you to find 3 interesting SEO opportunities for
"yourclient.com". These should be low effort high ROI
opportunities.

It doesn't guess. It pulls the domain overview, the keyword rankings, the referring domains, the page's own meta tags, the live SERP and real search volumes — then answers.

Low effort, high ROI is the important half of the prompt. You want three things a business owner reads and immediately understands — not a technical audit, and nothing that reads as a criticism of the site you built them.

3

Send this email

No deck, no attachment, no calendar link. Five a day is thirty minutes — two weeks is fifty clients.

The email 
Subject: something I noticed on [site]

Hi [name],

I was looking at [site] the other day — still looks great.

One thing stood out: [headline sentence from the brief].

Here are the three things I'd fix first:

1. [finding one]
2. [finding two]
3. [finding three]

I've started offering SEO alongside the builds, so I can take
care of this properly if you want. Either way the list above
is yours.

Want me to walk you through it on a quick call?

[you]
4

Quote a monthly price on the reply

Not a project fee. One page, three tiers, prices below — most people take the middle one, which is why there are three.

The one line not to cut: "either way the list above is yours." Giving the findings away unconditionally is what makes the email a favour instead of a pitch. And keep the offer to one sentence in the middle — never the subject, never a P.S. with a price in it.

The report is the one that matters. Every client who's been burned by an SEO agency was burned the same way — they paid for months and could never tell whether anything happened. Send it even in a flat month: say it was flat, say what you did, say what's next. Clients don't churn on bad numbers, they churn on silence.
